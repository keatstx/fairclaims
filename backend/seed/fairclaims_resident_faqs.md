**Q1: I got a huge hospital bill I can't pay — is there really free help?**

**A (Short):** Yes, for real — Illinois law says nonprofit hospitals have to give free care to families who qualify, and most people who qualify never hear about it.

**A (Full):** If your household income is under about $64,300 a year for a family of four, you may owe that hospital nothing — zero. That's the law in Illinois. Nonprofit hospitals are required to wipe out bills for families at or below 200% of the federal poverty level. The problem is hospitals don't advertise this. FairClaims finds the families who qualify, does the paperwork, and holds the hospital to what the law says. You don't pay FairClaims anything to do this.

**Sources:**
- [210 ILCS 89/ — Illinois Hospital Uninsured Patient Discount Act, Section 10](https://law.justia.com/codes/illinois/chapter-210/act-210-ilcs-89/) — Illinois law requiring free care for uninsured patients at or below 200% FPL

**Q2: Do I make too much money to qualify? I work and I'm not on welfare.**

**A (Short):** A lot of working families qualify — the income limits are higher than most people think.

**A (Full):** Under Illinois law, a family of four making up to around $64,300 a year can get their hospital bill wiped out completely. Even above that, the hospital can only charge you up to 20% of your annual income total — that's the cap. So if you make $45,000 a year, the most a hospital can collect from you in one year is $9,000, no matter how big the bill is. These rules were written for working people — not just people on public assistance. I can walk you through the steps, but I'm not a lawyer — for your specific case, FairClaims will connect you with a real advocate.

**Sources:**

**Q3: My bill already went to collections — is it too late?**

**A (Short):** It is not too late. Charity care applications can be retroactive, even after a bill goes to collections.

**A (Full):** Going to collections feels like the end of the road. It's not. Illinois law requires that hospitals accept charity care applications even after a bill has been sent to a collection agency. If you get approved, the hospital has to pull that bill back and zero it out. The key is acting now rather than waiting longer. FairClaims handles this kind of situation regularly — and they'll work directly with the hospital and the collector so you don't have to figure it out alone.

**Sources:**

**Q4: Will applying for charity care hurt my credit or get me in trouble because of my immigration status?**

**A (Short):** Applying for charity care does not hurt your credit, and it does not get reported to immigration.

**A (Full):** Applying for charity care is not the same as applying for a government benefit. It's asking a hospital to follow a state law they're already required to follow. It does not go on your credit report. Illinois law specifically does not require you to be a U.S. citizen or have legal immigration status to receive charity care — it only requires that you live in Illinois. Hospitals are not immigration enforcement, and FairClaims does not share your information with anyone outside the process of getting you help.

**Sources:**

**Q11: The hospital sent me a letter saying I don't qualify for charity care — what do I do?**

**A (Short):** Don't give up — denial letters can be wrong, and you have the right to appeal.

**A (Full):** Hospitals deny charity care applications for all kinds of reasons — some legitimate, some not. Sometimes the income was calculated wrong. Sometimes they asked for a document you could have substituted with something else. Sometimes the hospital just made a mistake. A denial letter is not the final word. FairClaims reviews denial letters and helps you file an appeal. If the hospital violated the rules in how they handled your application, the Illinois Attorney General has authority to hold them accountable. Getting a denial and stopping there is exactly what hospitals count on — don't let that happen.

**Q14: The debt collector keeps calling me. Can FairClaims make them stop?**

**A (Short):** Yes — there are federal laws that give you real power over how collectors contact you, and FairClaims can help you use them.

You can send them a letter telling them to stop contacting you — after that, they can only contact you to confirm they've received it or to notify you of specific legal action. FairClaims can help you send that letter, document violations, and connect you with legal advocates if a collector is crossing the line. I can walk you through the steps, but I'm not a lawyer — for your specific case, FairClaims will connect you with a real advocate.

# USE CASE 2: MEDICAL DEBT (Q21–Q40)

## STAGE 1: AWARENESS (Q21–Q25)

---

**Q21: Is it true some of my medical bills might just be wrong?**

**A (Short):** Yes — studies show up to 80% of medical bills contain at least one error.

**A (Full):** This is not an exaggeration. Research consistently finds that the majority of medical bills have mistakes — wrong codes, duplicate charges, services billed that were never provided, or charges for the wrong patient. On bills over $10,000, the average error is about $1,300. You have the legal right to request an itemized bill — a line-by-line breakdown of every charge — and to dispute anything that doesn't look right. FairClaims helps families do exactly this, and they know what to look for.

**Sources:**
- [Dialog Health — Medical Billing Statistics 2025](https://www.dialoghealth.com/post/medical-billing-statistics) — 80% of medical bills contain inaccuracies; bills over $10,000 average $1,300 in errors
- [HealthSureHub — Top Medical Billing Errors Statistics](https://healthsurehub.com/medical-billing-error-statistics/) — 49–80% of medical bills contain at least one error
- [CFPB — Medical Debt and Credit Reports](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-finalizes-rule-to-remove-medical-bills-from-credit-reports/) — CFPB explicitly connects the $88 billion medical debt burden to inaccurate and confusing billing practices

---

**Q22: I've been ignoring a collections letter about a medical bill — is that bad?**

**A (Short):** It's understandable, but ignoring it can make things harder — there are steps you can take right now that help.

**A (Full):** When you get a collections notice, you have 30 days to dispute the debt in writing — and if you do, the collector has to stop and verify the debt before contacting you again. That's federal law. Ignoring the letter doesn't make the debt go away, but it does let the clock run on your options. The good news: it's not too late to act, even if you've been ignoring it for a while. FairClaims can review the letter, check whether the debt is valid, look for errors, and figure out what your best move is. You do not have to just pay whatever it says.

**Sources:**
- [FDCPA — 15 U.S.C. § 1692g, right to dispute within 30 days](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/fair-debt-collection-practices-act) — Federal law giving you 30 days to dispute a debt after receiving a collections notice
- [FairClaims — Medical Debt Pillar](https://keatstx.github.io/fairclaims/) — "From debt verification to negotiation to connecting you with relief programs, we fight to reduce or eliminate medical debt"
- [Hyslip Legal — FDCPA Guide 2026](https://hysliplegal.com/blog/fdcpa-guide/) — Practical breakdown of consumer rights when receiving a collections notice

**Q93: What is a mass tort and how is that different from a regular lawsuit?**

**A (Short):** A mass tort is when a large group of people all sues the same company for the same harmful product or substance — you're not alone, and that actually gives you more power.

**A (Full):** In a regular lawsuit, it's just you against the company. That can be a tough fight when they have a team of corporate lawyers. A mass tort groups thousands of people with similar injuries — say, women who all used the same hair relaxer and got uterine cancer, or families who all had lead-contaminated water from a company's pipes — into one coordinated legal action. Each person still has their own case and their own potential payout, but the attorneys work together, the scientific evidence is shared, and the companies can't just wear one person down. In the hair relaxer case, there are more than 11,000 people in the federal case right now. In the Camp Lejeune case, there have been over 232,000 claims filed. Mass torts have resulted in billions of dollars in settlements for people who were harmed by products companies knew were dangerous. You don't have to fight alone.
